**Body**

On October 30, 2026, Britain's Competition and Markets Authority opened an investigation into commercial terms that Meta planned to impose on publishers and businesses using its platforms. Reuters independently reported the decision and the company's response. The probe is under the Competition Act 1998; opening it is not a finding of liability, a permanent prohibition or proof of market abuse.

The CMA says the proposed clauses could let Meta collect content from publishers' websites and use it to build products and services, including AI features, without a meaningful choice for the businesses creating that material. The regulator must assess whether Meta is dominant in the relevant British market, and only then whether the conditions are abusive.

Meta disputes that the inquiry is warranted and says it has already committed not to implement the changes without consulting publishers and other stakeholders. The regulator provides an important chronology: changes initially planned for October 30 were postponed to November 20 and then paused on October 28. It would therefore be incorrect to say the disputed provisions have already taken effect.

The case could affect bargaining power, remuneration for content and competition between platforms and news publishers, but the authority must gather evidence and hear the parties. The CMA has separately sought information on Meta One, a different subscription-related matter which should not be conflated with the new probe.
