Nel testo della proposta di legge di Bilancio 2027 arrivata alla Camera, ANSA e LaPresse individuano nuove risorse per la partecipazione italiana alle missioni internazionali. La somma esatta per il biennio è 1.323.579.339 euro, che i titoli arrotondano a oltre 1,3 miliardi.

La motivazione contenuta nel testo è l'evoluzione del quadro geopolitico internazionale. Questo fondo finanzia la partecipazione alle missioni estere, per la quale autorizzazioni, riparti annuali e controllo parlamentare contano tanto quanto l'importo stanziato. L'esistenza di una voce in manovra non specifica automaticamente quali teatri, contingenti o operazioni riceveranno ogni quota e non significa che nel 2026 siano già stati inviati nuovi soldati.

Nell'articolo 95 il disegno di legge contiene anche maggiori oneri per la convenzione sul Global Combat Air Programme, progetto con Regno Unito e Giappone per un futuro caccia: 32.338.720 euro nel 2027 e 15.563.360 nel 2028, secondo la ricostruzione LaPresse. Sono importi distinti dal rifinanziamento delle missioni e non devono essere sommati senza chiarire che si tratta di finalità diverse. L'articolo 94 prevede separatamente un fondo infrastrutturale per le Forze armate con 17 milioni per il 2027 e 87 milioni per il 2028.

Il dibattito parlamentare può modificare le cifre e le condizioni. Per valutare la priorità di bilancio è necessario confrontare le risorse annunciate con il costo totale della difesa, gli impegni già presi e gli altri capitoli di spesa. **Dek:** Article 95 proposes €520m for 2027 and €803.58m for 2028; these are proposed additions, not money already spent.

**Body**

In the draft 2027 Budget Law submitted to Italy's Chamber of Deputies, ANSA and LaPresse identify additional resources for Italian participation in international missions. On October 31, 2026, LaPresse cited Article 95, proposing €520 million in 2027 and €803,579,339 in 2028. The precise two-year sum is €1,323,579,339, commonly rounded in headlines to more than €1.3 billion.

The bill cites developments in the international geopolitical situation. The fund supports missions abroad, where specific authorizations, annual allocations and parliamentary oversight matter as much as the headline appropriation. The existence of a budget entry does not automatically identify which operations, deployments or locations will receive each share, and it does not mean new troops had already deployed in 2026.

Article 95 also includes additional sums for ratification of the Global Combat Air Programme convention, a planned future fighter project with Britain and Japan: €32,338,720 for 2027 and €15,563,360 for 2028, according to LaPresse. Those amounts are separate from the international-missions fund and should not be conflated without explaining their different purposes. Article 94 separately proposes a military infrastructure fund of €17 million in 2027 and €87 million in 2028.

Parliamentary debate may change the figures and their conditions. The government regards the appropriations as a way to sustain international commitments, but operational decisions would still require the relevant procedures. Assessing fiscal priorities calls for comparison with the total defence budget, existing commitments and other spending categories. The figures establish a multi-year funding proposal, not new missions individually authorized already.

**Facts**
- LaPresse reports €520m in 2027 and €803,579,339 in 2028 for the missions fund.
- The draft separately addresses GCAP and military infrastructure costs.
- The figures are proposed spending under parliamentary scrutiny.

**Perspectives**
- The draft invokes geopolitical developments to justify the resources.
- Parliament can amend the text and decide the final appropriations.

**Unknowns**
- This budget article does not specify the final allocation to each overseas operation.
- The parliamentary outcome and final appropriations are not yet known.

**Questions**

1. **How does the draft budget reach more than €1.3 billion for international missions?** — Article 95 proposes €520 million for 2027 plus €803,579,339 for 2028, totalling €1,323,579,339. They are additional proposed appropriations for future financial years, not money already spent on deployments.
2. **Is funding for the GCAP fighter included in the €1.32bn missions total?** — No. The GCAP amounts in the bill are separate costs related to the aircraft programme with the UK and Japan. Overseas missions funding should not be conflated with weapons development or military infrastructure appropriations.
